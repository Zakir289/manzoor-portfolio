Overview

OneTap is a simple physical interface designed to help older adults access everyday digital actions without navigating a complex smartphone.

Using familiar photographs and objects with NFC, a simple tap can trigger actions such as calling a doctor or family member, starting a video call, playing music, or opening digital content.

By turning familiar physical objects into simple touch points, OneTap makes everyday technology easier to access, understand, and use.

My Role

I worked across the end-to-end design process—from user research and problem definition to concept development, interaction design, physical prototyping and service design. I explored how familiar physical interactions and NFC could reduce digital complexity and support greater independence for older adults.

Problem & Solution

Problem

Older adults can use smartphones, but everyday tasks become difficult when they require remembering multiple steps, navigating unfamiliar options, or knowing what to do next. This can lead to hesitation and dependence on family members.

Solution

Phonoframes turns familiar photographs into simple shortcuts. An NFC-enabled frame connects a physical photo to a predefined digital action, allowing users to access it with a simple touch.

IDEATION

Exploring many ideas to solve the problem

We explored multiple physical and digital concepts to help older adults access frequently used digital services in a simple, familiar and intuitive way. These ideas were explored through brainstorming, sketches and quick prototypes.

01 NFC Memory Book

Best for: Daily home use

Pros
  • Familiar and easy to understand
  • Supports multiple actions
  • Easy to personalize with family photos
  • Works without navigating an app
Cons
  • Need to carry the book
  • Pages can wear over time
  • Initial setup required

02 NFC Cards

Best for: On-the-go / travel use

Pros
  • Portable and flexible
  • Can be kept in a wallet/purse
  • Easy to replace or update
  • Good for specific actions
Cons
  • Cards can be lost
  • Need to manage multiple cards
  • Less contextual than a book

03 Voice Assistant

Best for: Tech-comfortable older adults

Pros
  • Hands-free and natural
  • Useful for multiple tasks
  • Good for visually impaired users
  • Can work across the home
Cons
  • May not work in noisy environments
  • Speech recognition can be difficult
  • Requires learning and trust
  • Regional accent issues
